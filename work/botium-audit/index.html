01 / Scope and approach

A growing business.
An incomplete security program.

Botium Toys combines an office, storefront and warehouse at one US location while serving online customers in the US and abroad. The review covers its stated security program: employee devices, internal network, business software, ecommerce, payment and customer data, legacy systems and physical assets.

The evidence is limited to the supplied risk assessment, control-category guide and completed checklist. “Yes” means a practice is reported in the scenario; it does not demonstrate that the control operates effectively. No interviews, configuration reviews, penetration tests or recovery tests were performed.

Clarifying the GDPR exercise

The report describes a customer-notification plan within 72 hours, so the course item is marked Yes. Under Article 33, the controller must notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of awareness, unless the breach is unlikely to risk individuals’ rights and freedoms. Article 34 generally requires informing affected individuals without undue delay when the risk is high, subject to its exceptions. The scenario plan alone does not establish that these duties are met.

04 / Proposed remediation

Reduce exposure.
Then prove resilience.

A proposed sequence for the fictional company, not work already implemented. Timelines and owners would need agreement with management.

First / Contain exposure

Access & sensitive data

Inventory and classify assets; assign data owners. Restrict payment and personal-data access by role and separate conflicting duties. Protect sensitive data with encryption and managed keys.

Proposed owners: IT manager and data owners.
Evidence to obtain: approved access matrix, access tests, data-flow inventory and encryption configuration review.

Next / Make recovery possible

Backups & recovery

Create protected backups and a documented disaster recovery plan. Agree recovery objectives, then test restoration and record the results. Strengthen password policy and centralize password management.

Proposed owner: IT operations.
Evidence to obtain: restore-test results, recovery runbook and policy-enforcement checks.

Then / Sustain oversight

Detection & accountability

Introduce intrusion detection with assigned alert responders. Schedule legacy maintenance and define escalation. Review breach procedures and retain working physical, firewall and antivirus controls.

Proposed owners: IT operations and privacy lead.
Evidence to obtain: alert exercises, maintenance logs and a breach-response tabletop exercise.

05 / Reflection and references

A checklist is a start.
The reasoning is the work.

The key distinction is between having a control and knowing that it works. Availability today does not prove recoverability after an incident; an existing password policy can still be inadequate; a notification plan can answer a course question without proving regulatory compliance.

Framework context

The source scenario uses the five-function NIST CSF framing. Current CSF 2.0 has six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. The proposed actions span these functions; this exercise does not claim a complete CSF implementation.
