01 / The challenge

MAKE THE DATA
MAKE SENSE.

A list of coordinates does not tell much of a story on its own. The goal was to make satellite heat observations easier to understand through a map, useful time filters, and a readable observation list.

Behind that interface, the data needs a consistent structure. Fetching the observations, handling timestamps, and filtering results are all part of making the experience dependable.

02 / The process

START SMALL.
THEN GO DEEPER.

The first approach used Streamlit and Folium to explore the idea. Loading and interaction concerns then led toward Flask and a simpler frontend.

That transition brought more control over the interface. The backend stays focused on requesting, parsing, and filtering observations, while the frontend gives people a clear way to explore them.

03 / The decisions

CLARITY IS
A FEATURE.

The map and observation list sit close together, connecting a point with its details. Time filters for 1, 6, 12, and 24 hours narrow the view.

Straightforward German copy keeps the interface approachable. The user should be able to explore the information without learning the vocabulary of the underlying API.

04 / Where it stands

A PROTOTYPE.
A LOT LEARNED.

Ignis currently focuses on exploring satellite heat observations. Automated notifications and broader environmental risk layers are ideas for future development.

The images here are application screenshots, not live observations. Satellite heat detections require interpretation and do not automatically confirm a wildfire.
