01 / The challenge

GIVE ATTENTION
A DIRECTION.

Controls, camera movement, and HUD information compete for a player’s attention. The challenge is to make the current state understandable while keeping interaction consistent.

That means thinking beyond where a label sits. The relationship between an input, the response, and the feedback is part of the interface.

02 / The approach

ONE INTERACTION.
THEN THE NEXT.

Larger systems become easier to reason about when they are broken into smaller behaviors. I use that approach to work through controls and the visual states around them.

The Orion aircraft model brings that work into three dimensions. The interactive showcase above uses the geometry from my Blender file, including the airframe, landing gear, and smaller surface details.

03 / The design question

WHAT DOES THE
PLAYER NEED NOW?

My current focus is information hierarchy, camera interaction, and clear visual feedback. Essential information should remain readable without taking over the scene.

The model viewer lets you inspect the aircraft from different angles. The presentation uses simplified studio materials for the web; the mod itself remains in development.

04 / Where it stands

STILL BUILDING.
STILL LEARNING.

Orion remains in development. This case study describes the direction and the interaction problems I’m exploring, rather than a completed feature list.

It is pushing me to think about behavior and visual design together, and to be deliberate about what the player needs at each moment.

Taking it forward

A control can work and still feel confusing. The feedback matters.
